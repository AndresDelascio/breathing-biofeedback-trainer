4 · Before you order

Easy to miss

  • Charge-only USB cablesMany cables carry power but no data, and no COM port appears. Use one you've synced a phone with.
  • USB driverThe board's USB chip is usually CP2102 or CH340. Windows 11 often installs it. If not, get it from Silicon Labs or WCH.
  • Loose headersCheap MAX30102 boards often ship with the pin header unsoldered. Buy pre-soldered or plan to borrow a soldering iron.
  • 1.8 V pull-upsSome MAX30102 boards pull SDA and SCL to 1.8 V. Buy one whose listing or schematic says 3.3 V I²C.
  • Breadboard widthWide 38-pin ESP32 boards cover every free hole on one side of a standard breadboard. A 30-pin board, or two breadboards side by side, leaves room for wires.
  • GY-6500 on 3.3 VFeed its VCC from 3V3, never 5 V. ASSUMPTION, not yet checked on this board: NCS is pulled high so the chip stays in I²C mode. If the firmware reports "GY-6500 not found", tie NCS to 3V3.
  • Belt cableUse stranded wire, twist the four wires together, keep it near 1 m, and add strain relief at the clip so tugging doesn't pull on the pins.
  • 3D printer accessPublic libraries and makerspaces often print for the cost of filament. Measure your boards with calipers before modelling the cradle and clip.
  • Cord study only: measure resistanceMeasure the cord segment with a multimeter. If it's far from 10 kΩ, swap in the resistor value closest to it. Rubber cord can't be soldered: use crimps or small alligator clips.
  • Upload stuck at "Connecting…"Hold BOOT on the ESP32 while the upload starts. See firmware/README.md.

6 · First power-on

Bring it up in this order

Never connect anything to 5 V or VIN on the ESP32. Its GPIO pins are not 5 V tolerant.
  1. Wire everything with USB unplugged.
  2. Set BELT_SOURCE near the top of firmware/esp32_biofeedback/esp32_biofeedback.ino: BELT_NONE for stage 1, the default BELT_IMU for stage 3, BELT_ADC for the cord study (pick R_fixed close to the measured cord resistance).
  3. With the multimeter, check there is no short between 3V3 and GND.
  4. Plug in USB and upload the firmware (firmware/README.md has Arduino IDE and PlatformIO steps).
  5. Open Serial Monitor at 115200 baud. Look for the ready message and JSON lines.
  6. Check that finger changes when you touch the sensor. With the GY-6500 fitted, resp_raw sits near 2048 when still and moves smoothly as you breathe. Look for GY-6500 ready at 0x68 at boot.
  7. Close Serial Monitor, start the app, pick the COM port and click Connect USB.
  8. Sit still. Heart rate appears after three beats. Without a belt, breathing rate shows —; with the cord, it calibrates over about 20 seconds plus three full breaths.
